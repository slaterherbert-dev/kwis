Unit 3 · Day 5

Take the Castle!

It is 1420. Your lord has ordered you to capture Castle Rochfort, the stronghold of a rebel noble. Its stone walls are about three meters thick, it has a moat, and its storerooms are full.

You have 1,000 soldiers and food for 8 months. Every choice you make uses up one month.

Castle Rochfort is made up, but every siege method in this game is real.

Round 1 · 1420 · No cannons

Castle walls

Round 1 · Result

The siege failed.

You were not a bad commander. Before cannons, a well-built castle was very hard to take. Most medieval sieges dragged on for months and ended with starvation, disease, a deal, or betrayal, not with broken walls. That meant a noble with a strong castle could defy even a king.

Round 2 · Result

The walls came down in days.

Cannons changed everything. In 1449–1450, French royal artillery retook castle after castle in Normandy in about a year. In 1453, Ottoman cannons broke the walls of Constantinople.

But first, the bill.

6 bronze cannons4,000 crowns
Gunpowder (saltpeter shipped from far away)2,500 crowns
Expert gunners, paid every month1,500 crowns
300 oxen and wagons to haul the guns1,200 crowns
Iron and stone cannonballs800 crowns
TOTAL10,000 crowns

The prices here are made up, but the pattern is real: a cannon army cost far more than any single noble could pay.

A rich noble earns about 1,000 crowns a year. The king can tax the whole kingdom.

So who can afford cannons? Only the king. And now no noble’s castle can stop him.

Your turn

Think it through

1. Why did your first siege fail?

2. When cannons arrived, who gained power: nobles or kings? Why?

Done

A new word for what you just saw: absolutism

When cannons made castles useless and armies expensive, power flowed to kings who could pay for them. Some kings claimed total power, answering to no one but God. That is absolutism.
